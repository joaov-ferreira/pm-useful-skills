Verdict

Northwind's catalogue cards are excellent on phones, but finding a product means working outside the screen: search sits two screens down and results load below the fold with no count.

  • the first screen shows one promotional product, not the catalogue;
  • after searching, the screen does not move, so people repeat the search;
  • from 768 px up, only a 78×18 px link opens a product.
In Krug's terms, the list already carries the visitor; the rest of the page still makes them think.

Findings

F01High WCAG 4.1.3Nielsen #1Norman · FeedbackPolaris · P3

Searching doesn't bring people to the results or tell them how many came back

Observed

  • After searching "boots" on an iPhone 13 the viewport doesn't move (scrollY 1042 before and after); the list starts 81 px below the fold.
  • The status next to the list always reads "Updated", whether 5, 12 or 0 products came back.

Why it matters

  • Nielsen #1: people must see the effect of their action; without it they search again or assume it failed.
  • WCAG 4.1.3: a status message exists but carries no result, so screen-reader users hear nothing useful.

Recommendation

  • After results load, scroll to the results heading (offsetting the sticky header) and move focus to it; respect prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Replace "Updated" with "5 products for 'boots'" in a polite live region; show active filters as removable chips.

Where: /search results list · component "Search results header".

Method and limitations

How it was evaluated

  • Expert review against WCAG 2.2 AA, Apple HIG, Shopify Polaris and the GOV.UK Design System, and the principles of Krug, Nielsen, Norman, Wroblewski and Fitts.
  • Browser measurements at {{VIEWPORTS}}: target sizes, font sizes, contrast, reflow, headings, landmarks, form fields and Core Web Vitals.
  • Task walk-through: {{PRIMARY_TASK}}, plus a keyboard-only pass.
  • Mockups are illustrative wireframes in the site's visual language, not final design.

Limitations

  • Anonymous visit only: account, checkout and payment flows were not audited.
  • Chromium emulation, not real Safari or physical devices; no screen-reader session.
